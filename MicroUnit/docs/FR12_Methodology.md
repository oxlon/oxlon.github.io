Standards applied (as FR10): no lagged dependent variable, no own-history forecast, no estimated residual AR; small-
sample HAC with t(n−k) inference; fixed effects estimated exactly on unbalanced panels; with only five years per
panel, inference on the reported coefficients uses the **wild cluster bootstrap by year** (Webb weights, run, B = 499)
and Driscoll–Kraay p-values are not reported; every specification and anchoring choice made on data before the cut,
with an untouched hold-out; a rule is a procedure re-applied at every origin, so the hold-out and the forecast use the
same anchoring and the same fallback; Theil U against both a random walk and the constant; DM/HLN tests paired across
units (too few target years for a test across years — stated); the coherence rule where the training sample has at
least two transitions per unit (otherwise reported as not applied); combination with the null when the structural
model does not win; anchoring on the last residual (the FR1–FR10 add-factor convention) with each rule's implied
random-walk weight reported; plausibility of rates, flows and stock growth against each unit's own history;
arithmetic identity checks labelled as such. Entry and exit *rates* are bounded ratios with no stochastic trend and
births are modelled in logs with unit effects over five years, so FR12 has no long-run level relation: DOLS and
Engle–Granger are not applicable (stated, not applied for form).

## 1. The task

> *Rəqabət mühitinin təhlili, sektorlarda rəqabət intensivliyinin və müəssisələrin rəqabət strategiyalarının
> qiymətləndirilməsi və proqnazlaşdırılması mümkün olmalıdır.*

Agreed recommendation (Tövsiyə – D.Ə., 24 Aug 2026): FR12 delivers (1) measurement of **competition intensity by
sector** and of the **frequency of entry and exit**, with forecasts; (2) **scenario analysis** of sectors' likely
reaction to a specific policy or market change, based on industrial-organisation (IO) theory, with assumptions shown;
(3) detection of sectors where competition is **deteriorating**, through early-warning indicators; plus analysis and
forecasting of **market concentration**. Indicators are agreed with the Customer subject to data availability — hence
the data-source matrix (§5) and the gaps table (§6). Constraints: no AR/ARIMA/ARCH/GARCH, no lagged dependent
variable, no own-history forecasting; structural models showing related-sector effects; DSK data where the workbook
lacks it; NFR1 accuracy against simple benchmarks using only data available at the forecast time.

### 1.1 Why firms' "competitive strategy" is not estimated, and what replaces it

A competitive strategy — cost leadership, differentiation, tacit collusion, entry deterrence, predation — is a
**management intention**. No statistical source records it, and inferring intentions from aggregate data would be
speculation presented as measurement. Competition authorities monitor the **observable behaviour** in which
strategies show up, and FR12 does the same:

| Strategy-level question | Observable that FR12 measures | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Are incumbents deterring entry? | entry rate, size of entrants, licences issued | §8, §16, Layer B |
| Is the market selecting out weak firms? | exit rate, Kaplan–Meier survival by cohort, size of exiters | §8, Layer B |
| Is there rivalry for customers? | market-share instability, rank mobility | §8, Layer B |
| Is market power being exercised? | price-cost margin, Boone profit elasticity, margins rising while entry falls | §8, §16, Layer B |
| Is the market structure tightening? | HHI, CR4/CR8, size-class concentration bounds | §9, §14, Layer B |

## 3. Data collected

DSK publishes the entrepreneurship tables for the **latest two years only** and the statistical register for the
**latest period only**. FR12 therefore recovers **earlier vintages of the same DSK files** from the Internet Archive
(`web.archive.org/web/<timestamp>id_/…`, which returns the original bytes), checks that each is a genuine Excel file,
stores it once in `data/dsk_competition/vintages/` and re-reads it from disk afterwards. This turns two-year tables
into a 2019–2024 activity panel (without 2021, which no archived vintage covers) and gives full-year register flows by
NACE section for 2021, 2024 and 2025. FR10's downloads in `data/dsk_enterprise/` are read, never modified.

## 9. Concentration bounds from size-class data

Without firm data, HHI and CR4 are not observed, but they are **bounded** by what is published. Let size class *c*
hold $n_c$ enterprises with total output share $S_c$; each SME class has a per-firm cap $u_c$ = its statutory revenue
ceiling (micro 0.2, small 3, medium 30 mn AZN) divided by market output.

- **Lower bound.** For a fixed class total, $\sum_i s_i^2$ is minimised by equal shares: $HHI \ge \sum_c S_c^2/n_c$;
  $CR4_{low}$ is the sum of the four largest shares under that split.
- **Upper bound (headline).** $\sum_i s_i^2$ is convex, so on $\{0 \le s_i \le u_c, \sum_{i\in c} s_i = S_c\}$ its
  maximum is at a vertex: $\lfloor S_c/u_c\rfloor$ firms at the ceiling and one with the remainder. The large class
  has no ceiling, so its contribution is at most $S_L^2$: one large firm holds the whole class and the others are large
  by **employment** (> 250) with negligible revenue — the statutory definition is "employees > 250 **or** revenue
  > 30 mn AZN", so this cannot be excluded. $CR4_{up}$ is the matching vertex ($S_L$ plus the three largest capped
  firms), so the CR4 and HHI upper bounds describe the same extreme distribution.
- **Assumption-based variant.** If every large firm is large **by revenue** (revenue ≥ a floor *f*), the upper bounds
  become $(S_L-(n_L-1)f)^2+(n_L-1)f^2$ and $CR4 \le S_L-(n_L-4)f$ (trade: CR4 ≤ 4.2% at *f* = 30 mn AZN instead of
  33.8%). This is an **assumption**, shown with *f* = 30 and 15 mn AZN as sensitivity, and is not the headline.

Counts: active SMEs by class and activity (entrepreneurship `005`, the same population as the output shares `012`);
large units from the register (`1_3`). Where the implied average SME revenue exceeds its class ceiling (the output base

## 11. The structural entry and exit model

Two panels carry a time dimension: the **activity panel** (11 groups × 2019, 2020, 2022, 2023, 2024; registered
entrepreneurship subjects) and the **region panel** (14 regions × 2021–2025; statistical units).

**Entry is modelled as a flow.** Log new registrations (creations) with unit fixed effects and no lagged dependent
variable, $\ln B_{it} = a_i + \sum_k b_k x_{k,it} + \delta\,\mathrm{brk}_t + u_{it}$; the entry *rate* then follows
from the stock-flow identity, $e_t = B_t/N_t$ with $N_t = (N_{t-1}+B_t)/(1+x_t)$. The rate therefore falls as the
stock grows unless births grow as fast — which a model of the rate with a constant unit mean cannot reproduce (the
artefact found in review: a constant 2019–2024 mean rate sat far above the falling recent rates). **Exit** stays a
rate model (deaths scale with the stock). Activity panel: `brk` = 1 from 2022 captures the DSK definition change in
006 ("newly created / liquidated" up to 2020 → "newly registered / deregistered" from 2022, F17); the exit equation
carries a 2022 impulse (F16), because exit levels in 2023–2024 are close to 2019–2020 and no persistent break is
evident. The agricultural 2020 registration wave (F7) is excluded.

Candidate drivers — activity: sector demand growth (FR1 real VA of the group's sector), sector size (log real VA), the
lending rate, real credit growth (FR1) and the group's price-cost margin proxy; region: regional industrial output
growth and size (FR10), non-oil GDP growth and the lending rate (FR1). National drivers vary only over time, so year
effects cannot be added. With five years, Driscoll–Kraay p-values are unreliable and are not reported; the table gives
the **wild cluster bootstrap** p-value (by year, Webb weights, null imposed, B = 499) — coarse with five clusters. The
estimates are descriptive.

## 12. Pre-cut selection, untouched hold-out and both benchmarks (NFR1)

A **rule** is a procedure — driver set, anchoring, mode (structural / combination with the null / null) and the
coherence filter — re-applied at every origin on data up to that origin. The hold-out and the forecast use the
**same procedure**, so the forecast is exactly the rule that was validated (no fallback is introduced after the
hold-out).

- **Selection** (data ≤ 2022 only): estimate on years ≤ 2022, score 2023. Candidates: every driver set, unanchored
  (fixed-effect level) or anchored on the last residual (the FR1–FR10 add-factor convention). The best candidate
  replaces the null if its loss is lower and a paired test across units rejects equal accuracy at 10%; if lower but
  not significant, the forecast is the equal-weight combination with the null; otherwise the null. An anchored null
  is the random walk itself and is reported as that benchmark; each rule's **implied weight on the last observation**
  is reported (1 for an anchored rule, 0.5 for a combination of an anchored model and the null, 0 otherwise).
  Shrinkage of unit-specific slopes is not identified with two or three pre-cut years: pooled slopes (κ = 0), fixed
  before the cut.
- **Coherence rule**: a driver whose within slope lies outside the 95% CI of its first-difference estimate is
  dropped — applied only where the training data have at least two transitions per unit. With one transition (the
  region panel at the 2022 origin) the check is vacuous and is recorded as **not applied**, not as passed.
- **Hold-out** (never used for a choice): activity — origins 2022 and 2023, target 2024; region — origins 2023
  (targets 2024, 2025) and 2024 (target 2025). Realised FR1/FR10 drivers (conditional forecasts, as in FR10); every
  constant re-estimated at each origin. The entry **rate** is built from the forecast births and the exit rule's
  forecast deaths through the identity, starting from the stock at the origin (no realised endogenous outcome enters).
  Benchmarks: random walk (last observed value) and constant (unit mean over the training years). DM/HLN: with one or
  two target years a test across target years is impossible, so the test is **paired across units** (losses averaged
  by unit over overlapping origins); it ignores common year shocks and its p-values are indicative only.

## 13. Entry and exit forecasts 2026–2030 under FR1's three scenarios

The rules are applied at the last origin (activity 2024, regions 2025) with FR1's scenario paths and FR10's regional
output; the industry margin path, if a rule selects the margin, comes from FR10's section forecasts (the current
rules do not select it). Births are the median of the log model; the stock follows $N_t = (N_{t-1}+B_t)/(1+x_t)$; the
entry rate is $B_t/N_t$. For the activity groups 2025 is a nowcast (006 for 2025 is not yet published). Bands: 500
replications combining FR1's baseline draws, slope draws with sign rejection (acting on driver deviations, so unit
effects re-adjust), and **historical error pairs** $e_h = w(u_{s+h}-u_s) + (1-w)u_{s+h}$ (w = the rule's implied
random-walk weight), one pair per horizon, the same for births and exit and for all units, centred. Only 1–4
historical pairs exist per horizon (reported below), so band widths are coarse. To stop two close historical pairs
from producing a band that narrows with the horizon (it did for 2027 in v2.0), a horizon whose error set is less
dispersed than the previous horizon's (for births or exit) reuses the previous horizon's set, jointly for both
equations (v2.1). The historical error set therefore never becomes less dispersed with the horizon, and the
sharp pinches (e.g. births 32 % → 9 % → 19 % of the forecast in 2026–2028) are gone; flow and rate bands can still
narrow smoothly through the FR1 driver draws and the stock identity.

## 15. Scenario analysis with industrial-organisation theory

A calibrated, transparent Cournot toolkit. A market is summarised by its HHI (symmetric-equivalent number of firms
$N=1/HHI$), the demand elasticity ε at the calibration point and a conduct parameter θ (1 = Cournot, < 1 more
competitive). Industry Lerner index $L=\theta\,HHI/\varepsilon$; with price and output normalised to 1, marginal cost
$c=1-L$; linear demand $P=a-bQ$ with $b=1/\varepsilon$; symmetric equilibrium $Q=N(a-c)/(b(N+\theta))$.

| Scenario | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| (a) entry-barrier reduction / licensing simplification | $N \to N+\Delta N$ |
| (b) merger | $\Delta HHI = 2s_1s_2$; the HHI used is never below $s_1^2+s_2^2$; screens: US 2010 (1500/2500; ΔHHI 100/200) and EU (HHI 1000–2000 with ΔHHI ≥ 250, > 2000 with ΔHHI ≥ 150); Farrell–Shapiro: the price falls only if the merged firm's marginal cost falls by at least the pre-merger markup |
| (c) cost shock / unit tax *t* | pass-through $dP/dc = N/(N+\theta)$ under linear demand (= N/(N+1) under Cournot) and $N\varepsilon/(N\varepsilon-\theta)$ under constant elasticity — the same formulas as the code, checked numerically |
| (d) import competition / tariff change | competitive import fringe with share *m* and supply elasticity η: $L=\theta\,HHI_d(1-m)/(\varepsilon+\eta m)$ |
| (e) state-owned enterprise | **mixed oligopoly** (below) |

**Mixed oligopoly (De Fraja and Delbono 1989; partial privatisation as in Matsumura 1998).** One state-owned firm with
output share σ and increasing marginal cost $c+kq_0$ maximises $\lambda\pi_0+(1-\lambda)W$ (λ = 0: welfare-maximising
SOE; λ = 1: fully privatised); *n* symmetric private firms with marginal cost *c* play Cournot with conduct θ.
First-order conditions: SOE $P=c+kq_0+\lambda\theta bq_0$; private $P=c+\theta bq_i$. Calibration at the status quo
(λ = 0, P = 1, Q = 1): $c=1-\theta b(1-\sigma)/n$, $k=\theta b(1-\sigma)/(n\sigma)$ and $n=(1-\sigma)^2/(HHI-\sigma^2)$,
so the HHI used is never below σ² (and n ≥ 1). Assumptions: the SOE's marginal cost starts at the private firms'
level and rises with output; all state industrial producers act as one SOE. σ = state share of industrial output in
2025 (21.9%, DSK industry `010_2` via `FR10_ownership.csv`), manufacturing's 35.1% as sensitivity. The scenario raises
λ to 0.5 (partial) or 1 (full privatisation); the equilibrium is solved numerically.

Every scenario is reported as a **range** over the whole concentration range — HHI from the lower to the upper bound —
× ε ∈ {0.5, 1, 1.5, 2} (FR5's services system implies 1.0) × θ ∈ {1, 0.5}; the endpoints at ε = 1, θ = 1 are shown;
no "central" value is singled out. **Flagship markets** are calibrated from approximate public structure and are
**illustrative assumptions**, to be replaced by regulator data: mobile telecommunications (three operators, HHI
3,300–4,000), banking (about 22 banks, top five ≈ 60% of assets, HHI 800–1,300; CBAR concentration statistics to
replace it), cement (2–3 domestic producers plus imports, domestic HHI 3,500–5,500). Their revenue is not in FR12's
data, so their welfare effects are in % of market revenue only.

## 16. Early warning

Each flag compares a recent average (last three observations; exit: last two) with the earlier average, scaled by the
indicator's own year-to-year volatility in that sector (z), and is raised at |z| > z* in the adverse direction. Flags
in the composite: **concentration** (large-firm output share up), **entry** (log new registrations down — the flow,
so a growing stock cannot create a spurious fall), **exit** (exit rate up; 2022 wave excluded), **share mobility**
(industry only), **margins up while entry falls**; weights 0.25, 0.25, 0.15, 0.10, 0.25; score = weighted share of
raised flags among those available; watch list: score ≥ 0.30 or ≥ 2 flags; missing data give "insufficient data".
Information only, not in the composite: licences issued (rising issuance = more authorised entry, not a barrier), the
cohort-size ratio (not a survival rate), the state share of active SMEs (two years), inspections (F18).

**Threshold.** z* is the smallest value at which the simulated **false-listing rate** — the probability that a sector
with no change reaches the watch list — is at most 10% under both an i.i.d. and a random-walk null for every series,
with each sector's actual series lengths (20 000 replications per sector and null). The conventional z = 1 lists a
no-change sector a quarter of the time under the random-walk null.

## 17. Layer B — the firm-level competition engine (SYNTHETIC until the register arrives)

### 17.1 Input schema

One row per enterprise and year: `data_status` (synthetic marker; absent or any other value = REAL), `firm_id`
(pseudonymised, never the VÖEN), `year`, `nace2` (division, sections A–S and U), `region` (14), `ownership` (state,
municipal, private, foreign, joint), `size_class` (micro, small, medium, large), `revenue` (thsd AZN), `employees`,
`registration_date`, `liquidation_date` (empty while active), `status` (active; liquidated in the year of
liquidation); optional `legal_form`, `exports`, `product_codes`, `cost_of_sales`, `operating_costs` (for margins and
the Boone indicator; can also be linked from FR10's firm panel by `firm_id`) and `weight` (number of identical
enterprises a row stands for; 1 or absent in a census register). Full schema with Azerbaijani names:
`data/business_register/FR12_business_register_column_map.csv` and `output/FR12_business_register_schema.csv`.

### 17.2 Engine

Market = NACE division × region × year (and division × year nationally). HHI, CR4/CR8, entropy, number-equivalent,
Gini of revenues; share instability (½Σ|Δs|, entrants and exiters counted with zero share) and rank mobility
(1 − Spearman ρ of incumbents' ranks); entry, exit and churn rates and the relative size of entrants and exiters;
Kaplan–Meier survival by registration cohort (deaths in the registration year counted); the revenue share of firms
younger than five years; and, when cost fields exist, the price-cost margin and the **Boone indicator** — the
cross-sectional slope of log profit on log average variable cost per section-year (no profit-persistence AR model),
computed when at least 20 profitable firms (weighted count) exist. **Selection bias:** log profit exists only for
profitable firms, so loss-makers — typically the least efficient — are excluded; the sample is truncated on the
outcome and the slope is biased toward zero. The Boone indicator is read as a ranking across sectors and over time,
not as a structural elasticity; the price-cost margin includes loss-making firms. Every metric (HHI, CR4/CR8, entry
and exit, Kaplan–Meier, Spearman rank mobility, the Boone count) honours the frequency weight `weight` and treats a
missing column as weight 1; a swap test checks that a weighted file and its expansion to one row per enterprise give
identical results. The IO toolkit of §15 accepts the firm-level HHI directly.

### 17.3 The SYNTHETIC register

Seeded (`SEED + 17`); all NACE sections (A–S, U), 14 regions, 2019–2025. Births and deaths by section × region are
built by iterative proportional fitting and integer rounding that preserves both margins exactly, so the register
reproduces **exactly** the DSK births and deaths by NACE section where published (2021, 2024, 2025) and by region
(2021–2025), the end-2025 stock by section and by region, national-accounts output by section (as revenue), the SME
share of revenue by activity group and its micro/small/medium split (012), and — except where the 251-employee floor of large units binds — the SME share of
employees (013). Earlier stocks follow from births and deaths (the register's own stocks do not satisfy the stock-flow
identity, F5, so they cannot all be matched); the size-class mix is matched approximately to the 1 July 2026 snapshot.
Heterogeneity: large firms' sizes are Pareto with low shape (concentrated) in mining, utilities, telecom and finance
and high shape (fragmented) in trade and food service; a stated data-generating process links cost efficiency to size
so that the Boone indicator has something to find. Micro and small units are grouped into rows of up to 40 and 5
identical enterprises (`weight`) to keep the file small; the engine treats a row as that many enterprises.

## 19. Limitations

- Five annual observations per activity group and per region: selection and hold-out tests have very low power, the
  hold-out has one or two target years, the coherence check is weak (two to four transitions per unit) and the bands
  rest on 1–4 historical error pairs per horizon; the protocol limits the damage but cannot create information.
- The 2022 definition change in 006 is absorbed by one break term estimated from three post-break years.
- The activity panel counts registrations, including individual entrepreneurs, not market entry; register exit
  understates market exit (dormant units stay registered, F14).
- Activity groups are much wider than antitrust markets; Layer-A concentration is bounded, not measured.
- The IO scenarios are calibrations under stated assumptions, not estimates; the conduct parameter and elasticities
  are the main sources of uncertainty and are varied explicitly.
- Layer B runs on SYNTHETIC data: it proves the pipeline, not any fact about Azerbaijani enterprises.

### 20.3 Complete forecast table: section flows, SME share of employment and the component not forecast

**Register flows by NACE section** (statistical units, 2_1) are forecast by **allocation**, not by a model of their own: the
section flows are the same aggregate as the regional panel (in every full year the 2_1 section total equals the 2_3 regional
total), so the regional panel's births, deaths and stock forecast is the total; it is split first into the 11 activity groups
and then into the sections within each group, **anchored on the shares of the last actual year (2025)** (as in every module,
the last observation carries the level; in the 2024 → 2025 check the anchored shares are much more accurate than average
shares, above all for the stock). The 006 activity forecast is not used — it is a different aggregate (registered business
entities, F6). **2026**: the January–June 2026 actual is used as FR1 uses year-to-date data — full year = H1 2026 ÷ the
section's observed H1 / full-year ratio (2024–2025 average); the growth over the allocation's 2026 value applies in full in
2026 and then decays with a one-year half-life; the non-market sections O and U are excluded (228 administrative liquidations
in public administration in H1 2026, F10). In every year the sections are rescaled to the total: sections sum exactly to their
group and groups to the total; the bands are the total's band × the (adjusted) share. A section-specific driver rule cannot be
tested in a pre-selection design (≤ 2022 only 2021 is observed). January–June flows 2027–2030 = full-year section value ×
observed H1 / full-year ratio. In the catalogue these series are marked `derived: allocation`. **The SME share of employment**
is forecast with the same rule family as the SME output share (log-odds, group fixed effects, selection ≤ 2022 → 2023,
out-of-sample check, coherence filter in the last source year).
