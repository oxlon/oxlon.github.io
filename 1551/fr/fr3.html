Activity structure of GDP: five branches

FR3 · notebook FR03.ipynb · statutory sheet 2.4.1.3.

#Requirement (Tələb)

AZ, verbatim (MİİS TT §15.5.1, FR3): "ÜDM-in sahə strukturunun (artım və deflyator indeksləri) tempinin proqnozlaşdırılması."

English: "Forecasting the rates of the activity structure of GDP — growth and deflator indices."

Source: MİİS_TT_.pdf, pp. 36–40. Legal basis: Cabinet of Ministers decision No. 75 of 24.05.2004, clause 2.4.1.

Where FR2 gives the production method in eleven narrow sectors, this asks for the aggregated presentational view. The specification does not fix the number of branches, so the grouping follows the section headings of the statutory sheet itself.

#Model and why it was chosen (Model və seçim səbəbi)

This page estimates nothing, and that is the choice: a downstream notebook allowed to rescale would hide an upstream error, and the two published files would disagree. FR2's twelve components are taken exactly as written to the output contract, grouped into 5 branches with industry split into its oil-gas and non-oil legs, and the identity is checked in every year rather than closed. Under the previous architecture that check was empty, because FR2 scaled its sectors onto the aggregate FR1 had solved; with the scaling removed, the sector sum is the control total and FR1 reads it. The residual in the published file is three-decimal rounding.

Branch levels are direct sums; the branch deflator is a value-added-weighted average of FR2's sector deflators, and branch real growth is the residual of nominal growth and that deflator. The alternative convention — anchoring real growth, deriving the deflator — would give different numbers; the deflator was made the anchor so that FR2's estimated price channel passes through unchanged.

ADF is reported for each branch's derived growth series but gates nothing. With no structural equation at branch level the band cannot come from a fitted residual either: it is built from the out-of-sample error of a random walk on each branch's own history, widened by the same $\sigma_h=\sigma_1\sqrt{h}$ rule used elsewhere, and should be read as a lower bound on real uncertainty rather than an exact interval.

One definitional note matters for reading the figures: the industry-oil branch is the whole mining sector on the activity classification, while FR1's oil-gas block is only extraction plus its allocated net taxes. Aggregate GDP is untouched — the identity still closes at zero every year — but this is why branch deflators and growth rates differ from FR1's two-way split in some years.

#Reading (Şərh)

The structural direction is in the numbers rather than in any target: the oil-gas industry branch shrinks as a share of GDP while services remain the largest branch, and non-oil industry grows on the manufacturing, electricity and water equations behind it. The two industry legs move in opposite directions in most years — the split the review asked to see. Branch bands are wide, construction and net taxes especially, which reflects how the band is constructed.

#Backtest (Geriyə Sınaq)

FR3 estimates nothing, so it has no race of its own. The table carries the skill each branch inherits from its FR2 sector equations, and the benchmark rows g_act_agri … g_act_nettax in validation_backtest.csv that size the bands.

Reading: inherited skill is uneven — largest in construction, negative in agriculture — and the benchmark error is largest for net taxes and construction, which carry the widest bands above.
