#1Requirement (Tələb)

AZ, verbatim (MİİS TT §15.5.1, FR5): "Neftin dünya bazarında qiymətinin proqnozlaşdırılması."

English: "Forecasting the price of oil on the world market."

Source: MİİS_TT_.pdf, pp. 36–40. Legal basis: Cabinet of Ministers decision No. 75 of 24.05.2004, clause 2.4.1.

The shortest requirement in the specification and the one with the widest reach: the price is modelled once, written to one assumption key, and read from there by the GDP, trade, inflation and balance-of-payments blocks. No second price path exists.

#3Model and why it was chosen (Model və seçim səbəbi)

The price is modelled in logs: it is positive, shocks act proportionally, and residual variance stays stable across the level. Four candidates compete, and the random walk is not a courtesy benchmark — in commodity markets it is the theoretical expectation, since today's price already holds what is known about future scarcity. The race split in two, and both halves are published.

Information setResult
Unconditional — only past dataNothing beats the random walk. Drift variant −5% · mean-reverting AR(1) −8% · structural equation on lagged partner growth −34%
Conditional — current-year world growth treated as knownStructural equation wins, +21%, and takes all ensemble weight

The first half is the expected result: oil prices are not forecastable from their own past, and the sample drift is not different from zero. The second is the situation the package is in on the horizon, but the advantage comes from knowing world growth, so it is an upper bound and the WEO projection's own error is not in it. The supply side is not modelled: OPEC+ quotas, reserves and exploration costs appear in neither the workbook nor the template, and series are not invented. Geopolitical disruption enters the width of the fan, not the central path.

#4Econometric approach (Ekonometrik yanaşma)

The log price is non-stationary in levels and stationary in first differences, so all equations are built on Δln. The link between Brent and the Azerbaijani export price is tested only with Engle–Granger and is confirmed with an elasticity close to one — which is why one world price path suffices and the export price is not forecast separately.

Three observations — two global contractions and the recovery after the second — carry much of the slope. That is the content of the relationship rather than a weakness, and the sensitivity is shown both ways: a crisis dummy is insignificant and leaves the coefficient largely intact, while dropping the crisis years raises the neutral partner-growth rate and lowers the final-year path. Both variants sit inside the 50% band.

The fan is calibrated, not assumed: three error scales were tested against the fraction of actual h-year changes falling inside each nominal 80% band. The structural equation's own out-of-sample error is too narrow and the monthly-volatility scale too wide; the unconditional random-walk error calibrates closest and is published, widened by $\sigma_h=\sigma_1\sqrt{h}$ with accumulated parameter uncertainty from the HAC covariance.

#7Reading (Şərh)

The central path is almost flat, and the reason is in the coefficients: the partner-growth rate at which the equation leaves the price unchanged sits inside the range the WEO projects, so nothing pushes the price either way. Structural equation and random walk producing nearly the same path indicates stability. The band is deliberately wide — five-year changes in the sample range from a fall of more than half to a more-than-threefold rise — and the 50% band is the more workable planning frame.

#8Backtest (Geriyə Sınaq)

The brent_usd rows from validation_backtest.csv — benchmark, drift variant, AR(1), and the structural equation in both conditional and lagged forms — with the 80% coverage column.

Reading: on past information no candidate beats the benchmark, and that failure is published rather than hidden; the structural equation's advantage exists only when world growth is treated as known, and carries that condition wherever it appears.
