GDP production structure: eleven sectors, bottom-up

FR2 · notebook FR02.ipynb · statutory sheet 2.4.1.2.

#Requirement (Tələb)

AZ, verbatim (MİİS TT §15.5.1, FR2): "ÜDM istehsal strukturunun (artım və deflyator indeksləri, ümumi buraxılış, aralıq istehlak, əlavə dəyər) tempinin proqnozlaşdırılması."

English: "Forecasting the rates of the production structure of GDP — growth and deflator indices, gross output, intermediate consumption, value added."

Source: MİİS_TT_.pdf, pp. 36–40. Legal basis: Cabinet of Ministers decision No. 75 of 24.05.2004, clause 2.4.1.

For each of the 11 sectors of the statutory template: gross output, intermediate consumption and value added, each with a level, a real growth rate and a deflator.

#Model and why it was chosen (Model və seçim səbəbi)

Three things changed. Sector value added is no longer scaled onto a total decided elsewhere: the sectors are summed, the non-oil share of net taxes is added, and the result is published as non-oil GDP at market prices. Every sector gets its own named driver instead of sharing one of five, and a circularity guard rejects any driver downstream of non-oil GDP — which disqualified the previous ICT driver (non-oil GDP itself) and social-services driver (household income). Each deflator gets its own price term instead of a common inflation-plus-Brent right-hand side.

Nothing is accepted because it was proposed. A candidate must clear a gate — HAC p-value below 0.10 and the expected sign — then race the alternative, the previous driver and the benchmarks on one window. The backtest decides; losers stay in the catalogue marked rejected. Five of the newly named drivers failed the gate, and the alternative failed too in two of those sectors.

#Econometric approach (Ekonometrik yanaşma)

ADF is reported for every sector, driver and price series. The selected driver is built in three dynamic forms — static, lagged dependent variable, error correction — with error correction admitted to the central path only at twelve or more observations and confirmed Engle–Granger cointegration. All three go to the backtest ledger; the losing form is not deleted.

#Reading (Şərh)

Services and construction carry the bottom-up path: trade, catering and transport grow through the horizon on drivers with long samples, while mining declines with the oil-gas volume path. Because driver projections extend recent recovery rates, several sectors sit above their own long-run history, and the notebook flags them rather than trimming them. The sectors without a structural equation carry the widest interpretive uncertainty: their paths are benchmark ensembles with no economic channel behind them.

#Backtest (Geriyə Sınaq)

Rows from validation_backtest.csv: g_agri … g_social, d_agri … d_social, in both the candidate race and the operational race that sets the weights, plus *_dinamika.

Reading: named drivers win widely where the sample is long — trade +77%, tourism's catering alternative +84% — while sectors without one stay near their benchmark (ICT +4%, water +26%). Replacing Brent with the weighted index on the mining deflator is measurable: +78% against +62%.
