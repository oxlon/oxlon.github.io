Consumer price inflation

FR9 · notebook FR09.ipynb · statutory sheet 2.4.1.5.-7.

#Requirement (Tələb)

AZ, verbatim (MİİS TT §15.5.1, FR9): "İstehlak qiymətlərinin orta illik indeksinin (inflyasiya) təhlili və proqnozlaşdırılması."

English: "Analysis and forecasting of the average annual consumer price index (inflation)."

Source: MİİS_TT_.pdf, pp. 36–40. Legal basis: Cabinet of Ministers decision No. 75 of 24.05.2004, clause 2.4.1.

Two elements matter: the reporting unit is the average annual index, not a December-to-December point measure, because budget arithmetic and deflator chains use that concept; and the clause asks for analysis as well as a path, so the food, non-food and services split is tracked alongside the headline series.

#Model and why it was chosen (Model və seçim səbəbi)

The lead equation is an open-economy Phillips curve: lagged inflation for inertia, the log change in Brent for the cost channel, the log change in the nominal effective rate for pass-through, and a dummy for the two devaluations. The sample starts in the mid-2000s because the 1990s belong to a different monetary regime; re-estimating on the longer sample barely moves the coefficients, so the choice is not doing the work. The variant that knows the current year's oil price and exchange rate loses to the random walk in the backtest and does not carry the forecast; it stays as a structural reading. The lagged-driver variant beats the benchmark and joins an ensemble with a mean-reverting AR(1) and the announced target as a fixed anchor.

Inflation is stationary on both samples, so the equation needs no cointegration test; the change in the effective rate does not reject a unit root, which is stated rather than assumed away. That extension adds an output gap and real money growth after the specification was fixed; neither is significant and the adjusted fit falls, so the base specification stands.

#Reading (Şərh)

The central path is close to flat at 5.3–5.9 %, above the announced target because the sample's estimated equilibrium sits higher and the target anchor earned only part of the ensemble weight. The distance from the other readings is one of profile rather than level: the template sample starts far lower and rises, the IMF reference starts higher and converges on the target. The band is wide, because the devaluation and global price episodes sit in the residual variance rather than in extra dummies.

The fourth row is the one worth reading: an equation given the actual oil price and exchange rate of the year it forecasts still does worse than assuming no change, because coefficients re-estimated on a short training sample magnify oil-price swings. The equation is kept for interpretation and the benchmark family carries the path.
