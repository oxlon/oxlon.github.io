The balance of payments, closed

FR12 · notebook FR12.ipynb · statutory sheet 2.4.1.13.-14.

#Requirement (Tələb)

AZ, verbatim (MİİS TT §15.5.1, FR12): "Tədiyə balansının təhlili və proqnozlaşdırılması."

English: "Analysis and forecasting of the balance of payments."

Source: MİİS_TT_.pdf, pp. 36–40. Legal basis: Cabinet of Ministers decision No. 75 of 24.05.2004, clause 2.4.1.

Read literally, the clause covers the whole account, not the current account alone. This stage consolidates what FR7 and FR11 publish, adds the capital and financial accounts, and closes the BPM6 identity in every year, historical and forecast.

#Model and why it was chosen (Model və seçim səbəbi)

No coefficient is estimated here: the current account is the sum of two trade balances, the services balance and two income items, all estimated and tested upstream, so this stage's work is consolidation and closure. The capital and financial side, which the register carried as a gap, is now built out — capital account, four financial blocks, oil bonus, reserve assets, balancing item. Two blocks have an economic driver that clears the benchmark — repatriation follows export value, and inward direct investment follows the oil-gas investment cycle, if only narrowly. Portfolio and other investment are state-fund placement decisions with no macro driver, so benchmarks carry them. Reserve assets are derived from the identity rather than copied from the Ministry's workbook, whose vintage disagrees with ours.

CA+KA+FA+RES+EO=0\text{CA} + \text{KA} + \text{FA} + \text{RES} + \text{EO} = 0CA+KA+FA+RES+EO=0

The sign convention is the source sheet's own: financial-account lines are net inflows, and a negative reserve line means reserves rising. This requirement writes no row to the equation catalogue. Two derived quantities need no new estimation: the oil-price elasticity is recovered algebraically from the two scenario points FR7 publishes, the price equation being log-log with the same calibrated volume in both; and the break-even Brent price is found by inverting that function numerically, holding other components central — a simplification stated openly, since full pass-through would also move primary income.

#Reading (Şərh)

The current account falls from 3.1 % of GDP to -0.4 % as the oil-gas surplus covers less of a non-oil deficit worth about -14.6 % of GDP. The break-even calculation puts that in one number: the Brent price balancing the account rises from 62.90 to 75.96 USD/barrel, crossing the central Brent assumption (69.19) before the horizon ends. Reserve assets are the residual position, so the closing years of that line are an accounting remainder.

Two definition questions are open and published rather than decided. Repatriation is recorded twice from one profit flow — as a primary-income payment (3,031 mln USD) and as a financial-account line (-6,439 mln USD); they are not alternatives to be summed, and both are shown so the Ministry can confirm which its methodology means. And "non-oil current account" carries two concepts: the literal one used here, the current account less the oil-gas trade balance, and the source sheet's own line, which removes all oil-gas income flows. Four limitations stand: portfolio and other investment take no structural driver, the balancing item is zero from the first forecast year, reserve assets exclude revaluation, and the two workbook vintages disagree on the financial account in the last historical year — shown, not averaged.
