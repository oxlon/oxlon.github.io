Investment: structure and real growth

FR6 · notebook FR06.ipynb · statutory sheet 2.4.1.5.-7.

#Requirement (Tələb)

AZ, verbatim (MİİS TT §15.5.1, FR6): "İnvestisiyaların strukturu və real artım sürətlərinin proqnozlaşdırılması."

English: "Forecasting the structure of investment and its real growth rates."

Source: MİİS_TT_.pdf, pp. 36–40. Legal basis: Cabinet of Ministers decision No. 75 of 24.05.2004, clause 2.4.1.

Two layers: the structure — domestic/foreign, state/non-state, oil-gas/non-oil — and a real growth rate for each component. This page also carries the fiscal transmission channel behind the state leg.

#Model and why it was chosen (Model və seçim səbəbi)

Total investment is not re-estimated here. The credit-channel equation in FR1's core is estimated on the published total and enters FR1's demand equation as that same total, so the delivered path is the core's own path applied to the last actual level — a consistency requirement, not a preference. Foreign investment stays exogenous, its path set by the timetable of a few large projects that a univariate model cannot see; domestic investment is the residual. Four things changed, and two of the four returned results the review did not ask for.

#Econometric approach (Ekonometrik yanaşma)

Dependent variables are already real growth rates, so no level regression and no cointegration test is required; ADF does not reject a unit root in these short samples, hence HAC standard errors throughout. Each equation is also built in static, lagged and error-correction form, with the expanding-window test choosing between them and the losing form retained.

The devaluation dummy in the credit channel corrects a measurement break in the monetary aggregate, not a collapse in credit demand. The Ministry's own deflator form was adopted, re-estimated here and published unchanged; it does not beat the benchmark (−42%) and does not carry the path.

Three data facts are published rather than smoothed. The workbook's total investment row disagrees with the sum of its own components in the early 2000s by the same amount in all three splits — an older vintage in the total — so historical identity checks start where the splits become internally consistent. The two official sources for state capital expenditure disagree in two years; the merge fills only the empty years and prints the difference. And the template's nominal foreign-investment column implies a base well above the workbook's published level, so the real growth row is used instead: chaining the nominal column onto the actual level would have manufactured a false real increase.

#Reading (Şərh)

The delivered total investment path is slightly negative across the horizon, continuing the recent record — and it is the most assumption-sensitive number on this site: the credit-channel equation's zero-growth point sits inside the declared sensitivity range for real money growth, and in the upper third of that range the same equation gives rising investment. Giving the state leg its own equation has a visible consequence — since non-state investment is the residual, the state share rises through the horizon and the non-state component absorbs the whole difference. That is mechanical, it is not smoothed away, and the non-state profile should be read as the difference between two given paths.

#Backtest (Geriyə Sınaq)

Five races from validation_backtest.csv — total, foreign, state and oil-gas investment (the last as both a candidate race and a central-path row) and the deflator — with every losing model kept and its RMSE published.

Reading: the deflator race is decisive (+99%) and the total-investment ensemble beats its benchmark (+44%). Three negative results stand beside them: the two-term state equation, the fixed oil-gas share rule and the Ministry's own deflator form all lose to the random walk and carry no path.
