#2Model and why it was chosen (Model və seçim səbəbi)

The department's own equation catalogue — 92 rows, as received — is parsed and solved inside this package as a second runnable specification: its coefficients, its variable names, over our data layer. Two components make that possible: a parser for the EViews syntax it is written in, and a block-recursive Gauss–Seidel solver that resolves the system year by year. Describing the catalogue would have produced an opinion; running it produces numbers that can be raced. The results carry their own source label beside ours and never enter the central path.

#3Econometric approach (Ekonometrik yanaşma)

Nothing is re-estimated: the coefficients are used exactly as submitted. Four parser clarifications were needed and each is recorded in the code, the largest being that unparenthesised regime dummies had to be bracketed — otherwise a whole right-hand side collapsed into a single comparison and the equation returned zero growth in silence. One substantive change: the earlier engine extended drivers with arbitrary constants, and this module substitutes this package's published paths, so both specifications run on the same exogenous assumptions and any difference comes from the equations alone.

#6Reading (Şərh)

On the same exogenous assumptions the two specifications disagree in level more than in direction: the scenario runs inflation and non-oil wages lower and non-oil imports higher by the end of the horizon. Neither is offered as the better reading — the comparison that counts is the tested one below, available only where both forecast the same defined quantity.

#7Backtest (Geriyə Sınaq)

Both specifications are scored the same way: one step ahead, expanding window, drivers actual in the year forecast, random walk as benchmark. Two concessions are declared, both favouring the department: their coefficients are full-sample, so their forecasts are in-sample, while ours are re-estimated at every vintage; and our best form is entered for each series. A definition check drops any pair whose historical series differ by more than one standard deviation.

#9Status

This scenario has no row in the requirement register: it sits outside the FR1–FR12 list and is recorded as delivered extra work against review item A1. What is held is defined, not vague. 7 rows wait for the definition of the error-correction levels they reference, and one more runs on an assumed equivalence between two of them, flagged as such. 3 rows are blocked on four undefined balance-of-payments variables whose equations exist but whose data rows do not, and 1 row is prose rather than syntax. Supplying those definitions would move up to eleven further rows into the live set and remove the flagged assumption — the difference between running most of the catalogue and running all of it.
