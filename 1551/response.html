Response to the review

#1How to read this

The review of 14 August raised twenty-five items: four general and twenty-one on the equations. Each is set out below in the same four lines — what was said, which functional requirement it belongs to, what was done, and what the result was. Nine items are marked outside the FR list — delivered: the §15.5.1 requirement text contains no clause for them, and they were executed anyway rather than declined on scope. The result line is the verified outcome, including the cases where the outcome was that a proposed term did not survive its own test.

#2General items

#A1 — The module is not built on the department's own models

They said
the module uses the department's models only in part and consists of different equations, so its results need separate assessment.
Maps to
outside the FR list — delivered (the requirement text says what must be forecast, never whose equations must be used).
Done
the department's own equation catalogue was built into a fourth, runnable scenario — a parser for its syntax and a Gauss–Seidel solver over our data layer — published beside our path under its own source label, never merged into it.
Result
92 catalogue rows, 91 parse; 81 run live (66 exactly as submitted, 14 on documented substitutes, 1 on a flagged equivalence). 7 wait on undefined error-correction levels, 3 on undefined variables, 1 is prose rather than syntax. Published: 80 forecast rows and 164 backtest rows. FR13_nazirlik_spes.ipynb

#A2 — The comparison figures are not the Ministry's latest forecast

They said
those numbers are sample-model figures, not the Ministry's final forecast.
Maps to
outside the FR list — delivered; classified as our error and closed first.
Done
the mislabelled source value was retired across the whole output contract and replaced by a template-sample label (şablon-nümunə ssenarisi), with a separate, deliberately empty slot opened for the Ministry's official forecast. The vocabulary is enforced in the writer and guarded by a test.
Result
the source column now carries ours 12,703, template-sample 1,196 rows across 264 series, Ministry specification 80, IMF reference 15 — and official 0, as designed. No regression sees the overlay.

#A3 — How supply–demand balance is achieved is not visible

They said
it was not possible to determine how macroeconomic balance is secured in the system.
Maps to
outside the FR list — delivered in part (no expenditure-account clause exists in the requirement list).
Done
two independent readings of non-oil GDP were published — the production sum and the demand core — with no scaling and no forced reconciliation, and their difference published as its own series.
Result
the two readings differ by 3,016 mln AZN (2.8%) in 2026 and 25,532 mln AZN (14.3%) in 2030; the internal tolerance is breached in 4 of 5 years and the notebook prints the warning without correcting any series. Government final consumption is now published as well (36,182 mln AZN in 2030). The full expenditure identity is not closed, and the page says so.

#A4 — Net taxes should be forecast separately for oil-gas and non-oil

They said
net taxes on products should be projected separately for the two sectors.
Maps to
FR1, with FR3 as the branch-level home of the net-tax line.
Done
the split was made an explicit, calibrated, identity-checked parameter and both legs published as first-class series.
Result
oil-gas net taxes 4,145 → 4,402 mln AZN and non-oil 9,824 → 18,185 mln AZN, with five related identities passing in every year. A dedicated net-tax deflator equation was estimated, lost its backtest and was demoted to a benchmark ensemble — stated rather than hidden.

#3Items on the equations

#B1 — Non-oil GDP should be built by aggregation; industry split by oil/non-oil

They said
non-oil growth is computed by an equation; it should come from the aggregation of sub-sector forecasts.
Maps to
FR1 and FR2 (FR3 for the industry split).
Done
the architecture was inverted. The headline paths now come from the eleven-sector production sum plus the oil-gas block and the net-tax split; the old top-down demand equation is retained only as a diagnostic and is scored in the same table.
Result
non-oil growth 5.4% in 2026 is an aggregation output, and the bottom-up ensemble scores +46% against the random walk. The industry split is published: non-oil industry 6.0% and oil-gas industry -0.7% in 2026.

#B2 — Non-oil growth differs between FR01 and the final report

They said
the 2026–2030 non-oil growth rates differ between the two documents.
Maps to
outside the FR list — delivered (content-set unification, not a model defect).
Done
a version-stamped content pack now names, for every screen, its single feeding file, with a per-screen acceptance test and a named retirement list for the legacy content sets.
Result
one canonical set, stamp v3.4: non-oil growth for 2026 is 5.4% identically in the output contract, the notebook, the Excel workbook and the report. Two consecutive pipeline runs are byte-identical.

#B3 — FR01 does not fully characterise the expenditure side

They said
investment, final consumption and incomes are given, but the expenditure component of GDP is not fully characterised.
Maps to
outside the FR list — delivered in part.
Done
the expenditure legs that exist were wired in — government final consumption and household actual final consumption — and government consumption was tested as an independent driver of the non-oil core instead of being asserted.
Result
the government-consumption variant entered the core as an ensemble member with skill +44% against the random walk, without moving the published volume path; real government consumption is published (6.3% in 2026). The notebook states plainly that the full expenditure identity is not closed.

#B4 — Two different 2026 non-oil growth figures

They said
the guidance document and the scenario builder show different numbers for the same indicator.
Maps to
outside the FR list — delivered.
Done
the same mechanism as B2 — one version-stamped content set, both legacy sets named for retirement, a version stamp on every screen so the divergence cannot recur silently.
Result
the module's single 2026 non-oil figure is 5.4% with an 80% fan band; both quoted figures were traced to their legacy sources and put on the retirement list.

#B5 — Oil-gas growth must be built from volume × price

They said
the oil-gas growth formula uses the previous year's growth; it should use forecast production volumes, their domestic/export split and the corresponding prices.
Maps to
FR1 (oil-gas sector growth and deflator).
Done
a dedicated oil-gas block was built as a new pipeline stage. Oil-gas real growth is now a volume index over marketable oil and marketable gas valued at the previous year's prices.
Result
the equation is estimated on 2007–2025 with adjusted R² 0.971, and beats the random walk by +59%. The published path is now genuinely distinct from the template: -1.2% against -2.4%. Forward official volumes remain an exogenous assumption.

#B6 — The oil-gas deflator must use gas prices too

They said
the deflator should use weighted oil and gas prices; only an oil-price equation is visible.
Maps to
FR1 (oil-gas deflator).
Done
the Brent-only right-hand side was replaced by a value-share-weighted, chained oil-and-gas export price index plus the exchange-rate term, and the 2022 dummy was re-tested.
Result
the equation (2001–2025, adjusted R² 0.925) needs no dummy and scores +71% against the random walk, where the retired Brent-only form scores +64%. The weighted price index is published as a series and reused as the mining sector's price driver.

#B7 — Sub-sectors need sector-specific variables

They said
sub-sectors are forecast with a single approach, so sector specificity is lost; two named relationships are circular.
Maps to
FR2 (and FR3).
Done
the driver map was rebuilt. Every activity now gets its own candidate and, where one exists, an alternative; each passes the same significance gate, a stationarity check, a three-form dynamics race and an expanding-window backtest, with a guard that rejects any driver downstream of non-oil GDP.
Result
8 of 11 activities carry a sector-specific equation — trade at +77% and tourism at +84% against the random walk, among others — and 3 carry none, because every candidate failed the gate on the available sample. Both named circular dependencies are gone.

#B8 — Sector-specific deflator factors

They said
sector deflators are tied only to inflation and the oil price.
Maps to
FR2 (and FR3).
Done
each activity was given its own price term, raced against the previous form in the same window under the same gate.
Result
the new form beats the old in ten of eleven activities. Two counts are reported here and they are not the same count, which is worth stating plainly: the race is run over all eleven activities — including the ones whose price term does not pass the gate — and the new form wins ten of those eleven, tourism being the single loss. The gate is the separate, stricter test on the price term itself, and 9 of 11 terms pass it. Water and social services fail the gate, and power's term passes it but loses to the random walk — those three deflators stay benchmark-carried, with their weights published.

#B9 — Physical output indicators should be exogenous

They said
some product volumes are trend-forecast; since these data come from the responsible institutions they should be exogenous, with the trend kept as an alternative scenario.
Maps to
FR4.
Done
the principle was accepted and the channel for official paths already exists and is in use for two series. Pending the official plans, the worst of the trend problem was closed by reviving food-product rows frozen by #REF! in the template, re-based to the template's own level.
Result
the catalogue holds 157 product equations — 126 on a capped median-growth benchmark and 31 driver-based. Of the frozen food rows, 10 of 11 were revived, all of them beating the random walk and adding 480 backtest rows. One row was deliberately not revived: the template's unit and the statistical office's unit are different measures, which is a unit reconciliation question, not a concordance.

#B10 — Investment splits and budget investment

They said
only a domestic/foreign split exists; state investment should account for budget investment, and the non-oil state/non-state split is missing.
Maps to
FR6.
Done
the fixed three-year shares were replaced by backtested models on both cuts, the budget capital-expenditure channel was built into the state-investment equation, and the non-oil ownership split the review said did not exist was constructed.
Result
the oil/non-oil share rule is gone — the price-based ensemble leads at +15% while the old fixed-share rule scores −73% and is excluded. Budget capital expenditure is published (2,700 mln AZN in 2026), and the non-oil non-state leg is model-based and wins at +43%.

#B11 — The investment deflator

They said
the GDP deflator was used for investment; an investment-specific deflator is needed.
Maps to
FR6.
Done
two separate fixes. The genuine defect was conceded — actuals in current prices and forecasts in constant prices under one label — and the series were split into nominal and real twins with a price-basis column added to the dictionary. Then an investment-specific deflator was built on the Ministry's own concept: the construction deflator plus import prices.
Result
the price basis is now declared for all 641 dictionary rows and printed in the report. The deflator equation scores +99% against the random walk, and investment deflator growth is published at 2.1% in 2026. The notebook states that the relation is compositional, not a discovery.

#B12 — How wages are forecast, and the income channel

They said
wage forecasts exist but the method is unclear, and how wages affect household income is not visible in the model.
Maps to
FR8.
Done
the wage method was documented in a standalone note, and the missing channel was built — household income decomposed into wage bill, transfers and other income, cross-checked along an activity path and an aggregate path.
Result
the wage ensemble's strongest member scores +26% against the random walk. On household real income the decomposition wins the race (+45%) and the old direct rule is demoted (+24%) — wages now enter income as a right-hand-side variable and the change is measured, not asserted.

#B13 — Investment in the non-oil import equation

They said
the non-oil import equation uses consumption and the real effective exchange rate; investment should be added.
Maps to
FR7 (and FR11).
Done
a correction paired with a documentation fix — investment was already in the equation inside a composite absorption driver, but the catalogue's shorthand hid it, so the right-hand side was written out to name both components.
Result
the estimated equation runs on 2011–2025 with adjusted R² 0.205; two catalogue rows now carry the expanded right-hand side verbatim.

#B14 — Oil-gas imports: volumes and prices

They said
the oil-gas import forecast uses a three-year average; import volumes and prices should drive it.
Maps to
FR7 (and FR11).
Done
the premise was corrected first — the path is a flat hold, not a three-year average, because no candidate beat the benchmark — and then the missing data was collected: customs bulletin archives were unpacked and the relevant tariff headings extracted.
Result
in the race, the best alternative scores −5% — below the random walk — so the series is held flat at 2,526 mln USD and this is stated openly. The data gap is closed: 18 series over 2016–2025, each row provenanced, including the coverage break we found. The model gap stays open — a volume × price specification must win its own backtest before it can be published.

#B15 — Services by component

They said
services exports are forecast with non-oil GDP; they should be split into at least transport, travel, other business services and telecommunications, and by oil-gas / other.
Maps to
FR7 and FR11 (FR12 for the services balance).
Done
both directions were split into exactly the named components, each modelled on its own driver, and the bottom-up sum was raced against the aggregate equation on the same window; the reconciliation coefficient is published as its own series instead of silently scaling.
Result
6 component series are published in each direction. The bottom-up build wins on both sides — exports +34% and imports +43% against the random walk — and the aggregation identity is checked in every year.

#B16 — Gross income flows, repatriation, remittances

They said
only net primary and secondary income balances are forecast; the credit/debit split is needed, repatriation inside oil-gas payments is not shown, and personal remittances must be included.
Maps to
FR12.
Done
the gross credit/debit cross was modelled by instrument and by oil-gas / non-oil, then the gross build-up was raced directly against the net equation rather than assumed to be better; remittances were brought in from two independent sources.
Result
18 primary-income sub-series are published, with the difference between the two readings published as its own line. The net equation wins (+71% against +49%) and carries the headline. Remittances changed a result: on secondary-income debits, the remittance model now beats the random walk (+7%) where previously no model did. Repatriation ships under a provisional label pending the definition we asked for.

#B17 — Two different inflation figures

They said
the guidance document and the scenario builder show different 2026 inflation numbers.
Maps to
outside the FR list — delivered (a screen-vintage mismatch; the inflation requirement itself is complete).
Done
the content set was unified as in B2 and B4, and the module's own CPI method documented.
Result
the module's single figure is 5.3% for 2026, unchanged through every revision because the inflation block was never in the scope of the changes. The ensemble's target-anchor member scores +11% against the random walk; both legacy figures are on the retirement list.

#B18 — Fiscal parameters and the transmission channel

They said
fiscal parameters are absent, the transmission from budget spending to growth is not shown, and the non-oil base deficit ratio should be added.
Maps to
outside the FR list — delivered in part (the requirement list contains no fiscal clause).
Done
the fiscal block was wired into the data layer under label assertion, and three live transmission channels were connected rather than a fourth announced on paper: budget capital expenditure into state investment, real current budget expenditure and its infrastructure line as sector drivers, and social-fund transfers into household income.
Result
budget capital expenditure 2,700 mln AZN and transfers 12,983 mln AZN are published for 2026; the non-oil balance indicator is published on the state-budget perimeter only, historical, with the perimeter stated in its own label (-18,248 mln AZN). Forward budget paths remain data-gated, which is why the indicator has no forecast leg.

#B19 — Inertia and cyclical variation

They said
sector forecasts do not sufficiently reflect historical dynamics, inertia and cyclical variation; specification and calibration should be reconsidered.
Maps to
FR2 and FR3.
Done
the point was conceded with our own measurement, the metric was made permanent, lagged and error-correction dynamics were added wherever the backtest supports them, and constant driver assumptions were replaced by backtest-selected time-varying profiles.
Result
a published dynamics metric with one row per series, 43 rows in all; the dynamics race is published per activity, with error-correction winning in construction, lagged forms in two activities and static forms in four, while the three activities that carry no equation stay on dynamic benchmarks. The assumption set now holds 63 keys × five years = 315 rows, including driver profiles that were constants before. The internal acceptance check is not flattering and is printed as found.

#B20 — Nominal values in the report

They said
the final report should carry nominal levels beside real growth rates.
Maps to
outside the FR list — delivered (presentation).
Done
a nominal-level table was added beside the growth table on the report's first page, with the price basis printed per row so the B11 correction is visible in the same place.
Result
the report carries nominal GDP 141,482 → 214,908 mln AZN and the non-oil line 106,341 → 177,981 mln AZN, each row tagged with its basis. hesabat.pdf

#B21 — Flexible integration of official forecasts

They said
the model should allow official forecast indicators from the responsible institutions to be integrated flexibly.
Maps to
outside the FR list — delivered (governance).
Done
the mechanism was already architectural — one file is the single source of every forecast-horizon input, by construction — so the work was to write the contract that makes it usable: key, unit, supplying institution, effective date and source stamp, plus the empty official slot for the numbers themselves.
Result
the substitution contract ships with the content pack; the assumption set is 63 keys × five years = 315 rows, four official or exogenous inputs already enter this way, and the acceptance test for the scenario-builder screen states that no reference-only key may appear in the substitutable list — which is what makes an override safe.
