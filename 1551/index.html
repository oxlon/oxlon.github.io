MİİS §15.5.1 — Macroeconomic Analysis and Forecasting

#1Scope

This site is the representation layer of the §15.5.1 module: the twelve functional requirements of the Technical Specification (Texniki Tapşırıq), each traced on its own page from the requirement sentence through the model chosen, the equations estimated, the data used, the result published and the command that reproduces it. Every number on these pages is read at build time from the module's own output contract — forecast_long.csv, validation_backtest.csv, equations_catalog.csv, series_dictionary.csv and assumptions.csv — so the text and the model cannot drift apart. Where a requirement is served only in part, or not at all, the page says so, gives the measured reason and names what would close it.

The central paths are the module's own; the source column of the output contract keeps them separate from the statutory template's comparison overlay (şablon-nümunə ssenarisi, the template-sample scenario), from the Ministry specification scenario and from the IMF reference values. The bands are wide and widen with the horizon: by 2030 the 80% interval on non-oil growth spans both negative and double-digit outcomes, which is what a five-year interval estimated on annual data looks like. The central path is the module's best point estimate; the band is the range it cannot exclude.

#3Scoreboard

The twelve requirements were decomposed into 58 atomic sub-requirements, each traced to the Ministry data that serves it and to the notebook and series code that implement it. Of those, 42 are complete (TAM), 14 are partial (QİSMƏN, with the gap stated) and 2 are gaps (BOŞLUQ, with the negative proof recorded). The standard is strict: a quantity derived only by identity, with no equation of its own and no row in the backtest ledger, is scored partial even where the number is published. The full table, with the verified reason on every row, is on the register page.

#4The twelve requirements

  • FR1 — GDP: real growth, deflator, oil/non-oil and state/non-state splits. Headline GDP is now the sum of the eleven-sector production block, the oil-gas block and the net-tax split, not a separate equation. 12 sub-requirements: 9 complete, 1 partial, 2 gaps — ownership-split real growth and deflator exist in no source.
  • FR2 — Production structure: output, intermediate consumption and value added by activity. Each of the eleven activities is raced against its own driver and against dynamic benchmarks. 5 sub-requirements: 3 complete, 2 partial — three activities carry no sector-specific volume equation, three deflators are benchmark-carried.
  • FR3 — Branch structure of GDP and the industry oil/non-oil split. Five branches plus net taxes, reconciled to FR2's sector sum in every year. 4 sub-requirements, all 4 complete.
  • FR4 — Physical output of the main product types, including excise goods. 157 product equations, of which 126 are a capped median-growth benchmark. 7 sub-requirements, all 7 partial: the paths await official production plans, and the template's own product sheet carries 272 #REF! cells.
  • FR5 — World oil price. On an unconditional information set no candidate beats the random walk; the structural equation carries the central path conditionally on partner growth, and the fan is built from the random walk's out-of-sample error. 1 sub-requirement, complete.
  • FR6 — Investment structure and real growth rates. Fixed shares were replaced by backtested models on both cuts, with the budget capital-expenditure channel wired in. 6 sub-requirements: 4 complete, 2 partial — the second state-investment term the review asked for was tested and lost, and the non-oil state leg is derived by identity with no backtest row.
  • FR7 — Goods and services trade, structure, balance of payments. Services were split into the six components the review named, in both directions. 5 sub-requirements: 4 complete, 1 partial — oil-gas goods imports are held flat because no candidate beat the random walk.
  • FR8 — Household incomes, expenditures and their growth rates. Income is decomposed into wage bill, transfers and other income, and the decomposition wins its race. 5 sub-requirements: 4 complete, 1 partial — the sector wage shares do not clear the random walk, which therefore carries them.
  • FR9 — Consumer price index, annual average. An ensemble of an AR(1) term, the central-bank target anchor and a lagged Phillips form; skill +14% for the strongest single member against the random walk. 1 sub-requirement, complete.
  • FR10 — Average annual manat rate against the dollar and the euro. The statutory concept is the period average, not the end-of-period value; both rates are now read from the central bank's own table. 2 sub-requirements, complete.
  • FR11 — Trade and balance-of-payments indicators and structure. The same request as FR7 read a second time; served by the same notebook plus the balance-of-payments structure block. 3 sub-requirements, complete.
  • FR12 — Balance of payments: analysis and forecast. Current, capital and financial accounts, with gross primary-income flows and remittances published as their own series. 7 sub-requirements, complete.

#5What was added beyond them

The list above is the requirement list, and it is closed at twelve. Work delivered beyond it — the Ministry specification run as a scenario, the fiscal block, the backtest ledger as a shipped artefact, the data collected where inputs were not supplied — is set out on its own page, with what each addition produced and where it can be opened: what was added. It is kept apart from the twelve so that nothing here is counted as a requirement that is not one.

#6Running the module

python3 run_all.py                 # 15 stages over 14 notebooks, in DAG order
python3 run_all.py --stage FR09    # re-run a single stage

The pipeline stops at the first failing stage rather than continuing on stale inputs, and two consecutive full runs are byte-identical (compared by MD5). The Excel workbook and the results JSON — the two save formats the sub-task list asks for — are rebuilt from the output files as a post-stage step, so neither can ever contain a value the contract does not.
