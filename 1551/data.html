Data

#1What the module reads

The module has three input layers. The first is the Ministry's own material: the main statistical workbook (Statistik data dinamika 05.06.2026 +.xlsx, vintage 05.06.2026) and the statutory template, 8 vərəq (the six-sheet Decree-75 reporting template, 8_vereq_original.xlsx). The second is data the project collected itself, from published sources, because the indicator the requirement needed was not in the delivered material. The third is a frozen IMF reference extract, read-only, published for comparison and barred from entering any equation. Every read from an Excel file is label-verified — the loader asserts that the row label it finds matches the label it expects, so a shifted row fails the build instead of silently changing a number.

#4Provenance

Provenance is recorded per series, not per file: public_sources_provenance.csv holds 239 rows and customs_hs27_provenance.csv a further 18, each naming the source file, table, row label, source unit, published unit, year span, observation count and retrieval date (2026-06-03). Where a collected series and a delivered series overlap, the overlap is compared and the comparison published; where they disagree, the disagreement is published rather than resolved by preference. Series that remain unavailable are recorded as data gaps in the notebook that needed them, with the search that failed to find them written down.

#5What we still ask for

The original data request had 17 rows. After a full search of the delivered material, the statutory template, the main workbook and the published statistical sources, most of those rows were found and withdrawn; the request now stands at 6 rows, each of which either exists in no source or can only be closed by the Ministry's confirmation.

  1. GDP by ownership — real growth rate and deflator. The decree's clause asks for state and non-state growth and deflator indices; the statutory template's own sheet offers only the oil / non-oil split. Ownership shares exist, and ownership volume indices exist for retail trade, catering, paid services, investment and wages — but neither a growth rate nor a deflator for GDP or gross output exists anywhere. The request is at national-accounts level, historical and forecast.
  2. Approval details for the official forecast release. The numbers themselves are not requested: the paths are already filled to 2030 in the Ministry's own workbooks. What is requested is the approval date, document number and approving institution for the macroeconomic aggregates, the oil and gas production and its domestic/export split, the gas export price (plus its contract or market structure, since no hub benchmark series exists in any source), the product-group production plan for a named priority set, the state and consolidated budget paths with their consolidation and non-oil-balance methodologies and the medium-term expenditure framework, the expenditure components of GDP, and the State Investment Programme for the later horizon years.
  3. Wage and employment by 19 activities — the latest actual year. The activity series are in the model to the year the statistical tables end; the workbook's final column is marked "expected". Requested: the actual figures on the statistical office's definition and the annual update rule, confirmation of the employment definition, three short back-histories, and the productivity level index (the sheets carry only its growth rate).
  4. Activity turnover and tax receipts, back-history. The tax-administration sheet begins in 2021, which shortens several sector driver samples. The earlier period is requested on the same activity taxonomy.
  5. Two balance-of-payments definitions. The official definition and separate line for the repatriation component inside oil-gas primary-income debits, and its relation to the financial account's own repatriation line — the indicator currently ships under a provisional label. And confirmation of the definition and methodological continuity of personal remittances, which are already in the model from two independent sources.
  6. The Ministry specification: 7 error-correction levels and 4 variable definitions. Seven error-correction terms are used on the right-hand side of the department's equations but defined nowhere in the catalogue; four variable names cannot be traced to any series in the workbooks. One catalogue row is written as prose rather than as syntax and may be a transcription defect. Without confirmation these equations stay out of the scenario and the omission is stated beside the results, because the results will carry the Ministry's name.

The two rows that bear on the schedule are the second and the fourth: the second governs the official path for the fiscal block and the product forecasts, the fourth governs the estimation window for the sector drivers. The full request, with the found-and-withdrawn rows and where each was found, is reproduced in the reply pack annex.
